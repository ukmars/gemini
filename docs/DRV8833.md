# The DRV8833 Motor Driver

The chosen driver carrier is commonly available. It loks like this:

![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/f37b7433-3c9b-4474-8277-218f87600af8)

## SLEEP input

The driver chip has a pin called nSLEEP. On these cheap carriers, that pin is connected to a pin usually called 'EEP'. I guess they ran out of space.

From the perspective of the driver chip, if this pin is left open, it is pulled down by an internal resistor and the chip will be in sleep mode. That is, you will not get any outputs to the motors.

To enable the driver, the nSLEEP pin must be pulled up to the motor Voltage, $V_M$.

BUT...

There are many version of  the carrier. They have slightly different markings and they may, or may not, pull up the nSLEEP pin. This picture, from a random AliExpress vendor shows a jumper on the rear. It clearly shows hat the jumper must be bridged to enable the outputs. There is a 47k resisor on the board that performs this function when the jumper is bridged.
![image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0e1121b8-8d4c-47e5-a38e-0efe8757ed6f)

Apparently though, there are boards that have this jumper already bridged on the board so the outputs are always active.

Note that, if you look at boards from Pololu and Adafruit, this pin may, or may not already be pulled high. The Pololu carrier puls it high, the Adafruit carrier does not:

 - Pololu: https://www.pololu.com/product/2130
 - Adafruit: https://learn.adafruit.com/assets/36870

To be sure  the motor drive is enabled, pull the nSleep ('EEP) pin high with a 47k resistor connected to the motor supply.
